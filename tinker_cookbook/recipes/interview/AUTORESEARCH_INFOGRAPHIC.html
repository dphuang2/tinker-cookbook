§ 03

Four things that didn't fit my priors

Most of the wins were unblocking the base model, not teaching it new behavior.
01

SFT actively damages base reasoning.

Even 20 records collapsed checkpoint cadence to 99% skip and tanked accuracy. Across four SFT recipes, four regressions. Prompt-only beat every SFT variant we tried.

02

The eval cap was a hidden bottleneck.

Lifting max_tokens from 8 192 to 24 576 gave +9 pp — by itself the biggest single lever in the whole journey. Most "failures" before this were truncated thinking, not bad reasoning.

03

Verbose hedging is load-bearing.

Terse "use three checkpoints" → 0.828 (−4.5 pp). The "is typical for a multi-step problem" softness keeps the cadence distribution non-degenerate. Prompt phrasing was worth percentage points.

04

Two identical scores are not signal.

The 0.890 / 0.890 coincidence early on looked like convergence; it was just luck at temp 0.6. Real decisions need 3+ samples, ideally 20+ for tight CIs.

§ 04

The final cadence is sharply bimodal

Half the rollouts skip the tool entirely; the other half hit the anchor at three checkpoints. The mode is the prompt — almost no recipe behavior in between.
~90% of mass in two buckets · the prompt anchor reproduces almost verbatim, or not at all.
§ 05

The final recipe is three sentences

No fine-tuning, no tool examples, no demonstrations. A system prompt and a suffix appended to every user message. That's it. 
SYSTEM_PROMPT = "You are solving competition math problems. You have access to a checkpoint tool for tracking progress."

USER_INSTRUCTION_SUFFIX = (
  " Think step by step, then write your final answer in \boxed{} format. "
  "Don't think for too long unnecessarily, especially when you have a "
  "reasonable degree of confidence. Use the checkpoint tool when it "
  "helps you organize hard multi-step problems -- three checkpoints "
  "is typical for a multi-step problem."
)
